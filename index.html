Algorithm — reactions & assumptions used
  • Indirect reduction: Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂, FeO + CO → Fe + CO₂. Direct reduction: FeO + C → Fe + CO. Split between the two paths set by rd.
  • Silica and manganese oxide partially reduce into the metal: SiO₂ + 2C → Si + 2CO, MnO + C → Mn + CO — the rest reports to slag.
  • Phosphorus is assumed to reduce essentially completely (no removal route in a BF): P₂O₅ + 5C → 2P + 5CO.
  • Sulphur partitions between metal and slag (basic slag favours retention as CaS); the split is a user‑set fraction, not calculated from thermodynamics.
  • Raceway combustion of the remaining coke carbon with blast oxygen: C + ½O₂ → CO. Blast moisture reacts as H₂O + C → CO + H₂, adding H₂ to the top gas and consuming extra carbon.
  • Limestone/dolomite calcine and release CO₂ directly: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂, MgCO₃ → MgO + CO₂.
  • Carbon in the hot metal is fixed at the target saturation %C; everything else in the coke's fixed carbon is consumed by direct reduction, moisture-gas reaction, or combustion.
  • Gas volumes/percentages are computed on a molar basis (ideal gas, 22.4 Nm³/kmol), which is why the CO/CO₂ ratio is taken from moles, not mass.
  • Simplifications: no flue-dust carryover, no separate ore LOI/moisture stream, Fe recovery to metal fixed at (100 − Fe-loss)%.
Mass balance closes to within rounding when the coke rate, blast volume and blast oxygen are consistent with each other — that consistency is the real design variable in a blast furnace.
